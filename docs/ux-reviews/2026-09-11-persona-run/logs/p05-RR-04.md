## 1. 5-second impression (written before I touched anything)

- **What is this page? What can it do for me:** Looks like a little browser tower-defense game called "Duck Defense" — a title, a tagline in a language I don't read, and a big play button. Not sure yet if it's a full game or a demo.
- **Who is this for — me or someone else:** Feels like it's for Vietnamese-speaking casual gamers, not me specifically, but the layout is simple enough that a foreigner could probably still poke around.
- **Would I trust it enough to type my email or phone in:** N/A really — there's nothing asking for that. If it did ask, I'd hesitate, it looks like a hobby/indie project, not a company site.
- **Three words:** *confused, curious, cautious.*

## 2. What happened, step by step

I opened the link and just looked first, like told. Title, a Vietnamese sentence I couldn't parse, a "CHƠI" button, two more buttons under it, and a small footer. My first instinct as always: scan for a flag icon or "EN" text before touching anything else — and there it was, immediately, top-left: a little radio toggle "VI / EN" right at the top of the page. I didn't even have to hunt for it, it's the very first thing in the layout. That was a nice surprise, usually I have to guess at a globe icon that turns out to be something else.

I clicked EN. Home screen flipped instantly: heading stayed "Duck Defense" (fine, that's a proper name, not meant to translate), tagline became "Hold the road. Let nothing through.", the button became "PLAY", the two others became "Choose a map" and "Workshop", footer became "1 of 5 maps open" and "Cores", and "Cài đặt" became "Settings". I thought: *good, that's one clean pass, let's see if it actually holds up everywhere else.*

Went into Settings. Language section already showed English selected. Under it, a "Sound" slider at 80%. I clicked it and pressed Home to yank it down — it read "Sound Off" / 0 right away. That was my other must-have (café, can't have this thing making duck noises), done in two actions.

Went back to home, then into "Choose a map". Five maps listed: Meadow (in progress), then Stone Gap, Mist Marsh, Windy Pass, Old Forge, all locked with "Clear X to open" messages, all in English. No stray Vietnamese anywhere in that list.

Went into "Workshop" (the upgrade shop) next — this is normally where I expect leftovers, upgrade shops tend to have the most text. Went through the whole list: Economy (Starting gold, Bounty, Interest), Damage (Sharper arrows, Stronger powder, Deeper freeze), Utility (Starting lives, Cheaper towers, Better resale), Unlocks (Bolt tower, Venom tower). Every label, every description, every "Needs X level Y" lock message — all English. Footer line "0 of 5,545 cores invested" and the cores explanation line too. I read every single line here on purpose because this is exactly where I'd expect one string to have been missed, and I found nothing.

Then I actually started a battle to check the in-game screen, since that's the screen most likely to have missed strings (enemy names, wave callouts, tooltips). Placed an Archer tower, its info card showed "Archer · Level 1", "Damage 12 → 18", "Upgrade / Sell" buttons — English. Called the next wave; the "Next wave" preview said "8× Grunt" — English enemy name too. Hint bar at the bottom: "Keys: 1 2 3 pick a tower · Space calls a wave · Esc clears" — English. Nothing sneaking through here either.

Last check: reloaded the page cold, no cache tricks, just hit the same URL again. Language was still English on first paint, and it had even remembered my half-finished battle ("CONTINUE — Meadow · wave 1 of 12"). Went back into Settings: language still English, sound slider still showing "Off". Both choices survived the reload.

Honestly the moment that stuck with me: I went in expecting to hunt for a hidden globe icon and instead the toggle was sitting right at the top, unmissed, in both places I checked (home screen and settings). And going panel by panel afterward looking for the "one Vietnamese word that got missed" — I never found it. That's not the story I expected to write.

## 4. Three words afterward

*Relieved, satisfied, mildly impressed.*

Would I come back to this page? Yeah, actually — once it was in English I could read absolutely everything, and nothing broke or reverted when I reloaded, which is usually where these things fall apart. Compared to my first three words (confused, curious, cautious), this moved pretty far in the good direction — the confusion is completely gone because the language toggle was exactly where I instinctively look for it, and my caution eased off once I saw the settings actually stuck after a reload.
